D1. Problem & success snapshot

A business-critical request/case/transaction capability has accumulated fragmented processes and integrations. The goal is incremental modernization without replacing the core in Phase 1 or interrupting normal processing.

Problem

Customers, staff, and partners rely on a capability spread across a stable core, supporting applications, tightly coupled integrations, spreadsheets, email, and manual steps. This obscures end-to-end status and ownership, weakens auditability and operational insight, and contributes to inconsistent handling: 10–12% of work items need manual intervention. Modernize incrementally to reduce avoidable effort and improve visibility, changeability, and resilience.

Key constraints

Core retained in Phase 1 Usable capability in ~12 weeks Business processing continues Protect and minimize sensitive data AI outputs reviewable and governed Support future channels and higher volumes No big-bang replacement

Assumptions to validate

  1. A representative work-item lifecycle and exception categories can be agreed across affected teams.
  2. The core can exchange the minimum information needed through at least one supported mechanism; confirm interfaces and data ownership.
  3. Operational records or a time-boxed sample can establish manual-intervention and turnaround baselines.
  4. Business/operations representatives can review AI outputs, with an accountable owner for approval and escalation rules.
  5. The 12-week release can be bounded to an initial usable capability rather than all channels and processes.

D2. Architecture options & decision

Decision context: retain the core, continue normal processing, protect sensitive information, and target an initial usable capability in about 12 weeks. Interfaces, lifecycle, first-release scope, data ownership, and baselines remain unconfirmed.

Option A · Workflow-led case orchestration

Introduce a workflow/case orchestration capability around the existing core. It coordinates an agreed lifecycle, provides shared status and ownership, routes exceptions to a human queue, and calls existing systems through replaceable adapters. The core remains authoritative for its current records.

Strength: Directly addresses fragmented hand-offs, distributed process knowledge, exception handling, and missing end-to-end ownership.

Trade-offs: Requires lifecycle and data-ownership agreement, feasible interfaces, coexistence planning, and operational ownership; overlapping rules could be duplicated.

Option B · API/event backbone; retain existing workflows

Introduce shared APIs/adapters and supported notifications between channels, existing applications, and the core. Existing systems keep their workflows; a consolidated status/read view and operational history are assembled from their records and interfaces.

Strength: Decouples integrations and limits early changes to process behavior, supporting new channels without core replacement.

Trade-offs: Workflow ownership and rules remain distributed. A consistent view depends on reliable identifiers, status data, and interfaces; integration and reconciliation operations are added.

Preferred direction · conditional

Recommend Option A for a narrowly scoped initial lifecycle, subject to validating interfaces and business ownership. It more directly addresses status/ownership gaps, distributed process knowledge, and manual exceptions.

Accepted trade-offs: added workflow capability, explicit process/data ownership, possible rule overlap, and coexistence work.

  • Keep the existing core authoritative for its established records.
  • Use replaceable adapters; do not assume a particular interface.
  • Keep consequential decisions with authorized staff; AI recommendations remain optional, reviewed, and governed.

Revisit if evidence changes the decision

  • Core/application interfaces cannot support safe, timely interaction: narrow scope or start with integration/status foundations.
  • No common lifecycle or process owner can be agreed: defer central orchestration.
  • Workflow rollout threatens the 12-week target or continuity: reduce scope or phase differently.
  • Reliable identifiers/status/history are unavailable: address reconciliation before promising full visibility/audit.
  • AI governance or review capacity is unconfirmed: omit runtime AI; architecture can proceed without it.
Decision is a recommendation for human approval, not a confirmed implementation commitment.2 / 6

D3. Target-state architecture

Workflow-led orchestration around the existing core; incremental rollout for one agreed lifecycle. Interface mechanisms, topology, and products remain to be validated.

Components & data ownership

ComponentResponsibilityOwnership note

Queue / operationsHuman exception review; health and agreed measures.Accountable operational owners TBD.
History / optional AIRecord transitions/review outcomes; optional classification/routing recommendation.History is separate from source records; AI is not a source of truth.

Integration & data

  • Use supported source interfaces; do not assume protocols or event support.
  • Keep authoritative records separate from orchestration state and operational telemetry/history.
  • Validate identifiers, status mapping, duplicates, audit/retention, and reconciliation.

AI & human control

  • Runtime AI is optional, not a Phase 1 dependency. If approved, suggest classification/routing only.
  • Use minimized permitted inputs and an approved service. Authorized staff review recommendations and own consequential decisions, overrides, and final actions.
  • Define uncertainty handling, escalation, review capacity, and monitoring. Omit runtime AI if governance/data permission is unconfirmed.

Security & resilience

  • Identity-based least privilege; protect data in transit/at rest; apply minimization, retention, and deletion rules.
  • Attribute workflow actions and human decisions; protect audit history.
  • Define timeouts, retries/idempotency, error visibility, and reconciliation against real interfaces.
  • Monitor failed/aged work and integration health; agree availability, recovery, and capacity objectives.
  • Roll out incrementally with tested coexistence and rollback so normal processing continues.

Validate before committing design details

Core/application interfaces and owners; lifecycle and record boundaries; initial scope and coexistence; volumes/service/recovery expectations; audit/security/AI policy and human-review ownership. Products, infrastructure, protocols, persistence, and topology remain unspecified.

Alignment to D2: Implements conditional workflow-led orchestration with replaceable adapters around the retained core, one initial lifecycle, explicit record ownership, human exception review, and optional governed AI.

D4. Focused POC / validation

A browser-only exception triage mock-up tests whether a workflow-style queue can make routing suggestions reviewable and record human decisions without connecting to the core.

Question & hypothesis

Question: Can a compact triage queue make synthetic exception work visible, offer an explainable route, and capture human acceptance or override without changing or connecting to the core?

Hypothesis: Explicit exception reasons, suggested routes, human controls, and decision history make the orchestration and human-control flow understandable.

Scope & safety

  • Five fictional synthetic records with transparent keyword rules that simulate suggestions.
  • No AI model, external service, paid call, network request, personal/production data, credentials, or live enterprise system.
  • Human acceptance or override is required; no business transaction is performed.
  • Decision history is browser-session-only and resets on refresh.

Run the prototype

Artifact: poc/exception-triage/index.html

Open the file in a modern browser. Accept one suggestion; for another case choose a different route and apply the override. Review the counters and Decision history. No server, install, or dependency is required.

What the test demonstrated

  • Five synthetic exceptions render with route explanations, including a general-operations fallback.
  • Accept and override actions update the final route and queue counters.
  • Decision history records case ID, action, route, and browser-local time.
  • A reviewed item cannot be submitted again through the queue controls; refresh resets state.

Repeatable test & success criteria

  1. Open the file; confirm five synthetic cases await review.
  2. Accept a suggestion; confirm reviewed/pending counts and one history entry.
  3. Override a different case; confirm override count and action are recorded.
  4. Try to review the first case again; confirm no duplicate decision entry.
  5. Refresh; confirm the initial state returns and decisions do not persist.

Success: simulation is labeled; human actions change visible workflow state; decisions appear in session history; no production system or external AI service is invoked.

Learning

The human review/override interaction and visible decision capture can be demonstrated without a core integration. This supports the workflow concept, not AI capability or production readiness.

The D2 direction remains conditional on interface, lifecycle, and data-ownership discovery. The prototype itself does not change that condition.

Limitations · not proven

  • Keyword simulation only: no model accuracy or recommendation quality was evaluated.
  • Synthetic cases do not establish real exception patterns, volumes, or operational behavior.
  • No authentication, authorization, durable/audit-grade storage, integration, retry, privacy control, or production monitoring.
  • No core/application interface, end-to-end workflow, scale, or security behavior was tested.
  • Business validation remains needed for lifecycle, exception types, owners, retention, security, service expectations, and contracts.

Handoff to D5

Prioritize lifecycle and interface discovery. Treat model quality, durable audit, production integration, security, and scale as unproven; retain human review and override.

POC evidence: human-reviewed interaction only; simulated routing rules, no AI model.4 / 6

AI-use reflection

AI helped structure the problem, generate and challenge options, shape the architecture narrative/view, and scaffold the POC interaction. The team must verify facts, interface feasibility, lifecycle/data ownership, security requirements, and targets. The POC used deterministic keyword rules—not a model—so it does not validate AI quality or production readiness. Architecture choice, risk acceptance, AI-runtime approval, and delivery commitments remain human decisions.

Delivery guardrails

  • Retain the core in Phase 1 and continue normal processing.
  • Keep scope incremental; validate feasibility against the 12-week expectation without promising it.
  • Protect/minimize sensitive data; use only approved data and services.
  • Keep consequential decisions human-owned; AI optional and governed.
  • Keep source records distinct from orchestration state/history.
  • Do not overstate what the D4 mock-up proves.
