# D5. Delivery & risk plan

## Now / Next / Later roadmap

| Phase | Outcome and key work | Dependencies / exit gate | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Now — confirm and de-risk** | Agree the first in-scope work-item lifecycle, exception types, process owner, status vocabulary, authoritative record boundaries, and human-review responsibilities. Inventory core/supporting-system interfaces and data quality. Establish baselines and agree success-measure targets. Define security, audit, privacy, and continuity requirements. Use the D4 mock-up to validate the human review/override workflow with representative users. | **Gate:** business and operations approve bounded initial scope; platform owners confirm a viable supported integration path; security/data owners approve the handling model. If not, revisit Option A scope or begin with the D2 integration/status foundation. | Business/process owner, core-platform owner, operations, security/data owners — named owners TBD. |
| **Next — deliver initial usable capability** | Plan and build a thin, incremental workflow-led capability for the approved lifecycle: orchestration state, human exception queue, replaceable adapter(s), and operational status/history. Test coexistence, failure handling, access controls, audit capture, monitoring, and rollback. Pilot with a controlled scope while normal processing continues. The scenario's **about-12-week initial usable capability expectation** is a planning constraint, not a delivery guarantee; agree the scope and delivery plan before committing. | **Gate:** demonstrate agreed user outcomes and measures; pass security/operational review; confirm the pilot does not interrupt normal processing; obtain business and operations approval to expand. Core stays in place and authoritative for its records. | Delivery lead, architecture, engineering, operations, business owner — named owners TBD. |
| **Later — expand based on evidence** | Review pilot measures and feedback; improve exception handling and integration coverage; add channels or lifecycle stages incrementally; address data reconciliation and operational capacity. Consider AI-assisted classification/routing only if permitted data, governance, measurable value, monitoring, and human-review capacity are established. | **Gate:** expand only when the previous scope is stable, outcome measures are understood, risk controls are effective, and capacity/security are validated. Reassess the architecture if interface or ownership assumptions fail. | Product/business owner, platform and operations teams, security/data governance — named owners TBD. |

## Top five risks and mitigations

| Rank | Risk and consequence | Mitigation / early trigger | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Core or supporting-system interfaces are unsuitable or unavailable.** Orchestration may not exchange reliable work/status data, threatening feasibility and the initial usable scope. | Inventory and test supported interfaces early with system owners; prove one thin, non-disruptive interaction before committing scope. **Trigger:** required identifiers/status cannot be read or safely updated through an approved interface. | Core/platform owner and integration lead — TBD. |
| 2 | **Scope or coexistence threatens the about-12-week expectation or business continuity.** Expanding the initial lifecycle or changing live routing too broadly could delay usable capability or interrupt normal processing. | Bound the first lifecycle and channel set; phase rollout, preserve existing paths, rehearse rollback, and require a business-approved continuity gate. **Trigger:** critical path depends on replacing core behavior, broad migration, or stopping normal processing. | Business owner and delivery lead — TBD. |
| 3 | **Fragmented ownership, identifiers, or status data prevent a reliable end-to-end view.** Users may see conflicting or stale status, and audit measures may be incomplete. | Agree source-of-truth boundaries, identifiers, lifecycle/status mappings, and reconciliation rules; profile sample data before implementation and label stale/unknown status. **Trigger:** source systems disagree or cannot provide accountable status/decision history. | Process/data owners — TBD. |
| 4 | **Security, privacy, or audit controls are inadequate for sensitive information.** Data exposure or incomplete decision history could make the capability unacceptable. | Complete security/privacy and audit review before pilot; minimize copied data, enforce least privilege, define retention, and test attributable logging and failure access controls. **Trigger:** unapproved data flow, unclear retention, or missing actor/action audit evidence. | Security, privacy/data governance, and audit owners — TBD. |
| 5 | **AI suggestions are treated as reliable decisions without evidence or sufficient human review.** Incorrect routing, sensitive-data misuse, or unreviewed consequential actions could cause harm and undermine trust. | Keep runtime AI off by default; separately evaluate an approved model using representative, permitted data and defined measures; require human review, override, escalation, monitoring, and audit before any limited pilot. **Trigger:** sensitive data would reach an unapproved service, reviewers cannot handle volume, or performance/uncertainty is not measured. | AI/data governance and operations owner — TBD. |

## AI-use reflection

AI assisted with structuring the problem snapshot, generating and challenging architecture alternatives, translating the selected direction into a target-state view, and drafting the small POC interaction. The team must verify the scenario facts, interface feasibility, lifecycle and data ownership, security requirements, and success targets with accountable stakeholders. The POC used deterministic keyword rules—not an AI model—so it provides no evidence of model accuracy or production readiness. The architecture choice, risk acceptance, AI-runtime approval, and final delivery commitments remain human decisions.

## Delivery guardrails

- Keep the core in place in Phase 1 and preserve normal business processing during modernization.
- Keep the first release bounded and incremental; treat the 12-week expectation as a planning constraint to validate, not a guarantee.
- Protect and minimize sensitive information; use only approved data and services.
- Keep consequential decisions with authorized people. AI is optional, governed, reviewable, and excluded from runtime until explicitly approved.
- Keep source-system records authoritative; clearly distinguish them from orchestration state and operational history.
- Do not claim that the D4 mock-up proves production integration, security, audit durability, scale, or AI quality.
