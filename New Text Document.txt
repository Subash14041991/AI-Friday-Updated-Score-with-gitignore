Key constraints are the conditions the solution must work within. They limit what the team can change and define what it must protect.
1. Keep the existing EHR; deliver an initial capability in about 12 weeks.
   The electronic health record system stays in place during Phase 1. The team must choose a manageable first release rather than attempt the whole modernization at once.
2. Continue normal patient care and referral processing.
   Doctors, staff and patients must be able to continue their work while the new capability is introduced. Changes cannot depend on stopping the existing process.
3. Minimize and secure patient information; keep actions traceable.
   Use only the protected health information (PHI) needed for the task. Protect it and record important actions so authorized reviewers can see what happened, when and who did it.
4. Govern and review AI-assisted outputs.
   If AI is used, its outputs need appropriate controls and human review. Responsible staff must understand enough about a suggestion to judge whether it is suitable. This does not require AI to be part of the running system.
5. Support interoperability standards and future partners.
   The solution should make information exchange between systems easier and support additional providers later. The team must first check what connections the existing EHR and partner systems actually support.
6. Provide a safe route to a person.
   If patient-facing automation cannot resolve an issue—or the issue needs human attention—the patient must have a clear way to reach responsible staff.
These are requirements from the scenario. Unlike assumptions, they are conditions the proposed solution must respect.

“Assumptions to validate” means things we believe may be possible, but must confirm before relying on them. Unlike constraints, they are not established facts.
1. A manageable first referral process can be selected.
   We assume the business can choose a limited starting scope—for example, one referral type with selected teams and partners. Clinical and operational owners must confirm what is included.
2. Selected systems can exchange essential referral details and updates.
   We assume the existing systems can share the information needed. IT teams and partner providers must check their interfaces, available data and permissions.
3. Teams can agree identifiers, statuses and responsibilities.
   Everyone needs a consistent way to identify a referral, understand statuses such as “received” or “scheduled,” and know who acts next. Business and IT representatives must confirm those definitions. Clinical records remain under their existing authority.
4. Records or a sample can establish starting performance.
   We assume there is enough reliable information to measure today’s rework, delays and visibility. Operations and reporting teams must check the data. Without a starting measurement, improvement is difficult to demonstrate.
5. Governance can define escalation and permitted AI use.
   We assume the responsible clinical, privacy and operational owners can agree when a person must intervene and what AI may do or receive. AI use remains conditional on those decisions.
If an assumption proves false, the team may need to change the scope, design or delivery plan. That is why these checks matter before committing to the solution.



Success measures tell us whether the new capability actually improves the referral process. They compare performance before and after the change.
Measure	What it means in simple English
Less follow-up / rework	What percentage of referrals need staff to chase information or correct problems? We want avoidable work to decrease.
Faster turnaround	How long does a referral take to reach an agreed milestone, such as scheduling? We want that time to decrease.
Clearer status and responsibility	For what percentage of checked referrals can staff see the current status and who is responsible? We want this percentage to increase.
Fewer incomplete hand-offs	What percentage of referrals remain unresolved beyond an agreed time? We want this percentage to decrease.
More complete action records	What percentage of checked hand-offs record what happened, when, who acted and the outcome? We want this percentage to increase.


The other columns explain how to use each measure:
- Definition / method: How it will be calculated.
- Baseline: Today’s performance—the starting point. Only the scenario’s 15–20% follow-up/rework figure is available, and its scope still needs checking.
- Target: The improvement the business agrees to aim for after measuring the baseline.
- Data source / owner: Where the evidence may come from and who will be accountable for measuring it. Proposed sources and owners still need confirmation.
For example, if 18 out of 100 referrals require manual follow-up, the rate is 18%. After introducing the capability, the team measures the same referral group using the same rules and compares the result.
These are planned measurements, not improvements already achieved. Also, receiving a referral, scheduling an appointment and completing care are different milestones—they must be measured separately.





The application is a local referral coordination workspace. It helps staff enter referrals, correct missing details, track hand-offs, record partner receipts and follow up on delays.
Records and action history are saved in the same browser. The application records actions performed outside it; it does not send referrals to hospitals or partners.
1. Add a referral
Click Add referral and enter:
- Referral ID — required.
- Specialty.
- Receiving provider.
- Responsible person — required.
If specialty or receiving provider is missing, the record is saved as Needs information. Once both are filled, it becomes Ready for hand-off.
2. Correct missing information
Click Edit details, fill the missing information and enter the person making the change.
The application checks the details again and updates the status. Details can be edited only before a hand-off is recorded, so changes cannot silently invalidate a recorded hand-off.
3. Record a hand-off
First, complete the actual hand-off through the approved external process. Then click Record hand-off and enter the responsible person and hand-off reference.
The application:
- Changes the status to Awaiting receipt.
- Records the time and reference.
- Sets a follow-up deadline.
- Increases the hand-off attempt count.
This button records the hand-off—it does not transmit anything.
4. Record partner receipt
When the receiving provider confirms receipt, click Record receipt and enter the responsible person and confirmation reference.
The status becomes Receipt recorded. This means receipt has been recorded by a person; the application does not independently verify it with the partner.
Receipt is different from clinical acceptance, appointment scheduling or completed care.
5. Handle an overdue referral
If no receipt is recorded before the deadline, the status automatically becomes Follow-up overdue.
The application uses real elapsed time. It also recalculates overdue status when the workspace is reopened. Staff can then:
- Record a late receipt.
- Add a follow-up note.
- Approve another hand-off attempt.
6. Approve and record a retry
Click Approve retry and enter the responsible person and the reason another attempt is appropriate.
The status becomes Retry approved. Staff must separately complete the external hand-off and record its reference. The application retains the same referral ID and increases the attempt count.
If a late receipt arrives after retry approval, it can still be recorded. That removes the need for another hand-off in this workspace.
The remaining use cases are:
Use case	What the application does
Add the same referral again	Retains one record and logs the duplicate.
Add the same ID with different details	Rejects the conflicting details, preserves the original and records the rejection.
Add a follow-up note	Saves an administrative note with the person and timestamp. The latest note appears on the referral card.
Change follow-up time	Follow-up settings accepts 1–10,080 minutes. The default is 30 minutes. Changes apply only to future hand-offs; existing deadlines remain unchanged.
Search records	Searches referral IDs, specialties and receiving providers.
Filter by status	Shows only records in the selected status. Filtering does not delete anything.
View workspace counts	Shows total records, records needing information, overdue follow-ups and recorded receipts.
Inspect action history	Shows when an action occurred, the referral, action, person and recorded details.
Export records	Downloads records, settings and history as a JSON file. Importing that file is not currently implemented.
Refresh or reopen	Restores saved records in the same browser profile, provided browser storage has not been cleared.
Add fictional examples	Adds three example referrals without replacing existing work. Loading them again does not create duplicates.
Another tab changes the workspace	Loads changes when no form is open. If a form is open, warns the user; stale saves are rejected until reload.
Saving fails	Reports the failure and leaves the existing records unchanged.
Saved data cannot be read	Reports the problem and preserves the stored data rather than overwriting it.


The status sequence is:
Needs information → Ready for hand-off → Awaiting receipt → Receipt recorded
If receipt is delayed:
Awaiting receipt → Follow-up overdue → Retry approved → Awaiting receipt
A late receipt can also move an overdue or retry-approved referral directly to Receipt recorded.
For a short walkthrough, add an incomplete referral, correct it, set the follow-up to one minute, record a hand-off and wait for it to become overdue. Then approve a retry, record the second hand-off and record receipt. Finally, refresh to show that the record and history remain saved.
The current scope does not include staff login, changing the assigned role, patient notifications, scheduling, clinical decisions, real system connections or a central shared database. Typed person names are recorded labels, and the local history is not a protected audit system.

1. **Install Python 3.10+ and Node.js 20+** on the new system. VS Code is optional; Codex is not required.

2. **Download or clone your GitHub repository**, open the project folder, and open a terminal there.

3. **Install dependencies** using PowerShell:

   ```powershell
   py -3 -m venv .venv
   .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r requirements.txt
   npm ci
   npm run install:browsers
   ```

4. **Create your environment file:**

   ```powershell
   Copy-Item .env.example .env
   ```

   Edit `.env` with your API key, base URL, available model IDs and model selection for each of the five agents.

5. **Place your scenario HTML** in the `input` folder—for example, `input/new-scenario.html`.

6. **Check the setup, then generate:**

   ```powershell
   .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/run_pipeline.py input/new-scenario.html --validate-only
   .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/run_pipeline.py input/new-scenario.html
   ```

7. **Review the generated files** in the printed `output/runs/...` directory: D1–D5 Markdown files, POC and its README, architecture diagram, and final HTML/PDF report.

8. **After reviewing the generated POC source, run its tests** and refresh the report with validation evidence:

   ```powershell
   .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/run_pipeline.py input/new-scenario.html --resume "output/runs/<actual-run-directory>" --execute-poc-tests
   ```

   Follow the POC’s README to launch and use it. Confirm the run status is **`complete`**.

For every subsequent scenario, repeat **steps 5–8**. Keep `.env` and your API key out of GitHub.
